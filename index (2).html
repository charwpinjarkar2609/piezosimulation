CHAMBER I

Theory

Piezoelectricity is the ability of quartz, PZT ceramics and PVDF polymer to produce electric charge under mechanical stress. Scroll, and the crystal responds while the world behind it squeezes with it.

Balanced charges

Inside the crystal, positive and negative charges sit in balance, so the surface shows no net charge.

Force shifts the lattice

Pressure pushes the ions out of place and creates tiny electric dipoles throughout the crystal.

Charge gathers on the faces

Opposite charges collect on opposite faces, creating a voltage between the two electrodes.

A circuit gives current

Connect a load and the charge flows. The harder and faster you squeeze, the more current you get.

Real-life uses

Lighters and igniters

A spring hammer strikes a crystal and the spark lights the gas.

Ultrasound imaging

Piezo transducers send and receive sound inside the body.

Energy harvesting

Floor tiles and shoe soles turn footsteps into small amounts of power.

Sensors

Microphones, knock sensors and pressure sensors.

Quartz clocks

A vibrating quartz crystal keeps accurate time.

Medical stimulation

Studied for bone healing and nerve or tissue stimulation.

CHAMBER II

Practical

A typical energy-harvesting chain: piezo element, rectifier, capacitor, load. Moving dashes show the direction of current.

Formula to calculate current

Q = d33 × F
I = Q / t = d33F / t
  • Q: charge (C)
  • d33: charge constant (C/N)
  • F: force (N)
  • t: time (s)
  • I: current (A)
  • PZT ≈ 400 pC/N

Example: F = 10 N, d33 = 400 pC/N, t = 0.1 s gives Q = 4 nC and I = 40 nA.

Electrical devices used

Piezo disc

Generates charge from force.

Bridge rectifier

Converts AC output to DC.

Capacitor

Stores small pulses of energy.

Voltage regulator

Keeps a steady output voltage.

Charge amplifier

Turns tiny charges into a measurable voltage.

Oscilloscope or multimeter

Measures voltage and current.
